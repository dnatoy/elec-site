Motor OCPD Selection

Branch-circuit short-circuit/ground-fault protection, conductors, overload, and disconnect sizing per NEC Article 430.

Motor Inputs

Motor Rating — NEC 430.248 / 430.250
Determines which Table 430.52 percentage row applies.
  • AC polyphase other than wound-rotor (default): standard fans, pumps, compressors, and general-purpose induction motors. This is the correct row for most motors even if the nameplate happens to say “Design B” — see next point.
  • Design B energy-efficient: only changes the result when using an instantaneous-trip breaker (1100% vs. 800% here — every other device type gets the same percentage as the row above). Don't pick this just because a motor happens to be Design B construction; most general-purpose motors are, without it changing anything on this row.
  • Synchronous: large synchronous motors, most often seen on big chiller/compressor applications, sometimes chosen for power-factor correction.
  • Wound-rotor: increasingly rare — older cranes/hoists or large pumps/fans that used wound-rotor starting for reduced inrush before VFDs became common.
This selector doesn't apply once a variable-frequency drive is added — elevators are the most common example, but any drive-fed motor qualifies. Conductors and the disconnect ahead of a drive are sized from the drive's rated input current (NEC 430.122, 430.128), not motor FLC the way this calculator computes them, and drive-specific listing/marking can further constrain the OCPD choice (430.130). Size a VFD-fed circuit from the drive nameplate and manufacturer instructions instead of this tool.
Overload Protection — NEC 430.32
Per 430.6(A)(2), overload protection is sized from the motor's actual nameplate current, not the Table 430.248/430.250 value used for conductors and OCPD. Leave blank to use the table FLC as a stand-in.
430.32(A)(1): motors marked with a service factor of 1.15 or greater, or a temperature rise of 40°C or less, may use a 125% overload setting. All other motors are limited to 115%. Check the motor nameplate to confirm.
Branch-Circuit Protection — NEC 430.52
Only use this if the base Table 430.52 rating won't allow the motor to start — an engineering judgment call, not a default. The increased rating is a hard ceiling (rounds down to the nearest standard size, not up).

Affects only the Maximum (430.52) figure below — the Recommended figure is unaffected.

Instantaneous-trip breakers (MCPs) have no 430.52(C)(1) Exception No. 2 allowance — field adjustment follows the 430.52(C)(3) trial-setting procedure instead.

Ampacity Basis — NEC 310.16
Suggested as aluminum once the OCPD actually protecting the circuit (Recommended, or the Exception No. 2 rating when that override is checked) reaches 100 A, unless you choose otherwise.
Raceway & Fill
Line conductors only (motor circuits carry no neutral). Defaults to 2 for single-phase, 3 for three-phase. Excludes the equipment grounding conductor, added separately below.
